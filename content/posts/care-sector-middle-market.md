Most care sector M&A activity gets talked about in terms of large transactions — regional groups, portfolio deals, private equity roll-ups. The coverage makes it sound like that is where the action is.

It is not where most of the action is.

The majority of care businesses in the UK are small to medium-sized. One or two care homes. A domiciliary care business with 80 to 200 service users. A founder who has spent 15 years building something real and is now thinking about what comes next.

These businesses are largely ignored by the main brokers. The mandates are not large enough to justify the resource. The owners get a brief phone call, a generic information memorandum template, and an instruction to wait while the business sits on a list.

## What sellers in this market actually want

When I talk to care business owners who are thinking about selling, the same things come up.

They do not want their business advertised publicly before they are ready. Staff find out. Residents find out. Referral partners start asking questions. Before any deal is done, the business has been destabilised.

They want to sell to someone who will look after what they have built. This is not always about price. A founder who has spent 20 years in the same community cares about what happens to the people in their care.

They want a process that is proportionate. A £2m care business does not need a six-month investment banking process with 40-page information memoranda. It needs a clear, confidential introduction to a small number of qualified buyers.

## What buyers in this market want

The buyers I work with — care groups, operators, smaller private equity vehicles — are not looking for businesses that have already been shopped to 50 other buyers. By the time a business is widely marketed, the best buyers have often already passed.

They want early visibility. A conversation before the seller has committed to a process. The chance to build a relationship before competing on price.

That is exactly what off-market origination provides.
